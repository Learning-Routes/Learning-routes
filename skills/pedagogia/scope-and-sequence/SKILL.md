Scope is what goes in; sequence is the order. Get both right on the map before any lesson is written, because reordering later means rewriting.

Scope:
- Keep only what serves the route's outcome. Extra topics become a short "para ir más allá" lesson at the end, or are left out. Suggest; the creator decides.
- Size for a short online route: about 3 to 6 modules, 3 to 5 lessons each. One main idea per lesson, 5 to 10 minutes of work.
- A lesson title that holds "y" twice ("Variables, bucles y funciones") is usually three lessons.

Sequence, in priority order:
1. **Prerequisites first.** If B needs A, A comes earlier. Check every lesson: "¿qué necesita saber antes?".
2. **Simple to complex, concrete to abstract.** Start with a case they can picture, then the rule, then the exceptions.
3. **Whole task early.** Let students do a small version of the final task soon (module 1 or 2), then grow it. Do not save all application for the end.
4. **Practice lesson at the end of each module** that uses the module's lessons together.
5. **Reviews later on**: plan where earlier ideas come back (see spaced practice).

How to work with the creator:
- When their order breaks a prerequisite, say which one in one sentence and propose the swap. Ask before changing it: their order may have a reason you do not know.
- Show the plan as module titles with lesson titles under them, short and in their words.
- Give the reason for an order only when you change theirs.

Never:
- Add topics the creator did not mention as if they had asked for them. Offer them, marked as your suggestion.
- Drop or rename their topics silently.
- Build lessons (add_blocks) during planning: titles only.
- Ask about scope, order and length in one message.

Example (creator lists "canciones completas" before "acordes"):
> "Para tocar canciones completas hacen falta los acordes, así que te propongo enseñarlos primero."
> ask_creator: "¿Pongo los acordes antes de las canciones?" with "Sí, cámbialo" / "No, déjalo así".

**Sources**

- Wiggins, G., & McTighe, J. (2005). *Understanding by Design* (2nd ed.). ASCD.
