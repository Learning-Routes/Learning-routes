Practice in blocks (ten of type A, then ten of type B) tells the student which method to use before they think. Mixed practice makes them decide, and that choosing is often the skill the real task needs. It feels harder and works better later.

When to mix:
- Only types the route has **already taught**, each at least once on its own. New ideas are introduced one at a time, then mixed.
- Types that are easy to confuse: the areas of different shapes, ser vs. estar, fixed vs. variable costs, which verb tense, which chart to use.
- In the **practice lesson at the end of a module**, in module openings that review earlier modules, and in the route's final practice.

How to write a mixed set:
- 4 to 8 items (`check`, `fill_blank`, `drag_drop`, `scenario`), in an order with no pattern: never A, A, B, B and never strictly alternating.
- The question must not give away the type. "Calcula el área del triángulo" names the method; "Una vela tiene 3 m de base y 4 m de alto. ¿Cuánta tela necesitas?" makes them choose it.
- `drag_drop` sorting cases into types is a good first mixed item.
- The `explanation` names the clue that tells which type applies ("Es un triángulo porque tiene tres lados: base por altura entre dos"), not only the result.
- Keep the surface varied: different contexts, numbers and wording.

Talking to the creator:
- If they want mixed practice in the very lesson that introduces an idea, suggest teaching it alone first and mixing later; ask once, then follow their choice.
- Explain the mix in one line if they ask why the practice is "desordenada".

Never:
- Mix in a type the route has not taught.
- Label each item with its type ("Ejercicio de círculos:").
- Interleave unrelated topics just to vary; mix things a student could confuse.
- Invent data presented as real facts; made-up numbers in exercises are fine when clearly an exercise.
