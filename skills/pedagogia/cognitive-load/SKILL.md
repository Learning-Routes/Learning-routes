Working memory holds only a few new things at once. A beginner who must juggle new terms, a long text and a busy picture learns little. Keep each block about one thing and remove what does not help learning.

Sizes for a short online lesson:
- One main idea per lesson, 5 to 10 minutes, about 6 to 12 blocks.
- A `concept` block: one idea, up to about 120 words, short paragraphs. Longer means two concepts with a check between them.
- At most 2 or 3 new terms per block; define each in plain words the first time it appears.
- A `tip`: one or two sentences. A `summary`: 3 to 5 key points.

Order and pacing:
- Alternate reading and doing: concept, check, concept, example, check. Never three text blocks in a row.
- Go from a concrete case to the general rule.
- Novices get worked examples before problems; experienced learners can skip them (see worked examples, faded examples).

Words and pictures:
- Use a `visual`, `video` or `motion` only when it shows something words explain badly (a process, a layout, a movement). Write a real `image_description`.
- Put the explanation next to the picture it refers to, in the same block, not a screen away.
- Do not repeat the same text in narration and on screen; pick one.
- No decorative images, background music or effects: they pull attention from the idea.

When the creator asks for more:
- If a lesson holds several ideas, propose splitting it into lessons, in one line, and ask once.
- If they want decoration everywhere, explain briefly that it distracts beginners and offer media only where it explains. Their decision stands.

Never:
- Cut, shorten or rewrite the creator's own text to fit these sizes. Their material goes in as they wrote it; at most, suggest where it could be split.
- Pack a whole topic into one `concept` block.
- Add jargon the lesson does not need.

Example (one idea, one block):
> `concept`: "La epidermis es la capa más externa de la piel. Nos protege del agua, el sol y los microbios." Then a `check` before the next layer.
