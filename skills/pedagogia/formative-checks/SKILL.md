A check inside a lesson is not a grade: it shows the student (and later the creator) whether the idea landed, and its feedback teaches. Frequent, short checks with good explanations do more than one test at the end.

How many and where:
- One `check` right after each `concept` it tests; 3 to 5 per lesson. Never two `concept` blocks in a row without a check, a `fill_blank` or an `example` between them.
- The last check before the `summary` should need the lesson's main idea.

Writing the question:
- Test the idea, not the wording: make the student apply, classify, predict or spot a case. If they could answer by finding the same sentence above, rewrite it.
- One clear question, in plain words, no double negatives, no trick.

Writing the options:
- 3 or 4 options, exactly one with `correct: true`, unless the question says "elige todas las que...".
- Wrong options come from real mistakes: the misunderstanding a beginner really has, the step most often skipped. Each must look reasonable to someone who did not get it.
- Similar length and style; the right answer must not be the longest or the only precise one. Vary its position.
- Avoid "todas las anteriores", "ninguna de las anteriores" and true/false when a richer question fits.

Writing the `explanation` (always fill it):
- Two or three sentences: why the right answer is right, and why the most tempting wrong one is wrong.
- Encourage the next try without praise padding.

The creator's own questions:
- Add them exactly as given: same question, options and right answer. Do not add, drop or reword options.
- If theirs has no explanation, ask whether to write one; do not slip it in.

Never:
- Invent a fact to build a question on, or quiz on something the lesson did not teach.
- Write a check whose answer is given away by the text right above it.

Example:
> question: "Ana deja 100 € al 10 % de interés compuesto. ¿Cuánto tiene tras dos años?"
> options: "120 €" (false), "121 €" (true), "110 €" (false)
> explanation: "El segundo año el 10 % se calcula sobre 110 €, no sobre 100 €: 110 + 11 = 121 €. 120 € sería interés simple."
