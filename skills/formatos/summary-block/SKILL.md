A summary block has `key_points` (a list of short texts), an optional `body` (one or two sentences before or after the list) and may carry a `title`. It closes a lesson.

What to do:
- Read the lesson's blocks first. Every key point restates something the lesson already taught, in the same terms the lesson used.
- Three to five key points. One idea each, one line each, starting with the idea itself: "La evaporación convierte el agua líquida en vapor."
- Keep the order the lesson followed, so the summary works as a map of it.
- Use `body` for a single line that ties the points together or says what comes next ("En la próxima lección veremos…"), only if the next lesson already exists in the plan.
- If the creator wrote their own summary, it goes in as they wrote it.

Never:
- Never add a fact, term or example the lesson did not cover. A summary is not the place for something new; if the creator wants a new fact, suggest a concept block before the summary.
- Never copy whole paragraphs from the lesson; condense them.
- Never pad with general advice ("Recuerda practicar mucho").

Example (a lesson on the water cycle that taught evaporation, condensation and precipitation):
> key_points:
> - "El sol calienta el agua y la evapora."
> - "Al subir, el vapor se enfría y se condensa en nubes."
> - "Cuando las gotas pesan, caen como lluvia: es la precipitación."
> body: "Estos tres pasos se repiten sin parar."

- Fiorella, L., & Mayer, R. E. (2016). Eight ways to promote generative learning. *Educational Psychology Review, 28*(4), 717-741.
