An example block has one field, `body`, and may carry a `title`. It goes right after the concept it illustrates.

What to do:
- Show one idea in one concrete case the student can picture: real objects, amounts, places or situations from their world. Ask the creator's context (who the students are, where they live, what they care about) only if you do not know it yet.
- For a procedure (a calculation, a recipe, a grammar rule), write a worked example: every step in order, each on its own line, with a short "why" for the steps a beginner would miss. Finish with the result stated plainly.
- When the idea is easy to confuse with another, a second example that is almost the same but where the idea does not apply helps the student see the boundary. Put it in its own block.
- Keep numbers simple, so the arithmetic does not hide the idea, and check every result yourself.
- Use the creator's own examples first. They know their students; their example goes in as they wrote it.

Never:
- Never invent anything about the creator or real people: no "una alumna mía, Ana, mejoró un 40%". If the creator wants a story from their classes, ask them for it. If you write an imagined case, it must read as imagined ("Imagina que…"), never as a fact.
- Never present invented figures as real data (prices, statistics, dates). Use round, clearly illustrative numbers or ask the creator.
- Never stack several ideas in one example.

Example (worked, for adults learning to budget):
> body: "Imagina que ganas 800 al mes.
> 1. Separa primero lo fijo: alquiler 300, transporte 50. Quedan 450.
> 2. Guarda una parte antes de gastar: 10 % de 800 son 80. Quedan 370.
> 3. Eso es lo que puedes gastar en el resto del mes: 370."
