Use this to plan, write or review a maths route.

**Where learners get stuck** (design each lesson against the one that fits):
- Fractions treated like whole numbers: 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5, or "1/8 is bigger than 1/6 because 8 > 6".
- Decimals compared by length: 0.25 > 0.5 "because it has more digits".
- The equals sign read as "now write the answer", so 3 + 4 = □ + 2 gets 7.
- Letters in algebra read as objects ("3a = three apples") instead of numbers that vary.
- "Multiplying makes bigger, dividing makes smaller", false for numbers below 1.
- Rules used without meaning: "move it across and change the sign".

**Exercises and blocks**
- example: a worked example with every step shown, then a near-twin for the student.
- check: wrong options taken from the real errors above; the explanation says why that path fails.
- fill_blank with match "exact" for numbers, formulas and symbols.
- drag_drop to order the steps of a procedure or to pair equivalent forms (0,5 ↔ 1/2 ↔ 50 %).
- simulation with a formula and sliders when watching a change teaches (slope, area, interest).
- cards for definitions and properties to recall later.

**Beginner sequence**: a concrete situation, then a picture or diagram, then the symbols. One idea per lesson: worked example, guided practice, then mixed practice that brings back earlier lessons.

**Pitfalls for you**
- Recompute every number, result and option before writing it. Exactly one option in a check is correct.
- When the creator's material has a wrong result, say it plainly with the right one, and ask before touching their words. E.g. "En tu ejemplo, 1/2 + 1/3 da 5/6, no 2/5: antes de sumar hace falta un denominador común."
- Follow the notation of the creator's country (decimal comma or point, ÷ or :). If unclear, ask.
- Stay at the students' level: no variables for ten-year-olds unless the creator wants them.
- Never invent how many students make an error, or any other figure.
