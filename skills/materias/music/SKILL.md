Use this to plan, write or review a music route.

**Settle first**: the instrument or voice, and whether students learn by ear, by reading scores, or both. Don't assume piano or guitar: ask, one question at a time.

**Where learners get stuck**
- Note values taken as fixed times ("a quarter note lasts one second") instead of parts of a pulse whose speed is the tempo.
- Reading symbols before the sound is familiar: they decode the score but cannot hear what it means. Sound should come before sign.
- Rushing or dragging; counting rests as silence to skip, not as time that passes.
- Mixing up note names across systems (C D E vs do re mi) and octaves.
- Practising only from the start of a piece at full speed, so the hard bar never improves.
- Tension and posture habits that later cause pain.

**Exercises and blocks**
- audio: listen first, then imitate, clap or sing back. Only with a recording the creator uploads; never invent an audio link. Add a transcript or description of what to listen for.
- video for posture and technique, only from the creator's own recording.
- cards for symbols, note names and terms (front: symbol or term, back: meaning).
- drag_drop to order note values by length or to match a symbol with its name.
- fill_blank to complete a bar so the beats add up.
- check for theory, with wrong options from the mix-ups above.
- interactive when the creator brings their own piano, metronome or game: it goes in as they made it.

**Beginner sequence**: steady pulse and simple rhythms with the body; then a few sounds on the instrument or voice by ear; then the notation for what they can already play; then short pieces combining both. Each lesson ends with a slow, focused practice task.

**Pitfalls for you**
- Explain rhythm in beats counted aloud ("1 y 2 y"), never in seconds. E.g. "Una negra dura un pulso; cuánto tiempo sea depende del tempo."
- Use the naming system the creator uses, and say which one when it could be ambiguous.
- Check every bar you write: the beats must add up to the time signature.
- Don't give medical advice about pain or the voice; suggest a teacher or specialist.
- Never invent facts about composers, pieces or performers.

**Sources**
