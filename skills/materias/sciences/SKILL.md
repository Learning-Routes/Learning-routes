Use this to plan, write or review a science route.

**Where learners get stuck** (students arrive with their own explanations; name them, then test them):
- Seasons caused by distance to the Sun. It is the tilt of Earth's axis, which changes the angle and the hours of sunlight.
- A moving object needs a force to keep moving; heavier things fall faster.
- Heat and temperature as the same thing; "cold" as something that flows.
- Plants getting their mass from the soil, not mostly from carbon dioxide in the air.
- Evolution as individuals changing on purpose during their lives.
- Matter disappearing when it burns or dissolves.

**Exercises and blocks**
- check: first ask what the student predicts, with the common idea as an option; the explanation shows the evidence.
- scenario: a situation where everyday intuition and the science disagree.
- simulation with a formula and sliders for relations between quantities (speed, pressure, concentration).
- drag_drop to order a process (a cycle, the steps of an experiment) or to pair cause and effect.
- visual with a described diagram; mark contains_diagram.
- cards for terms and units.

**Beginner sequence**: a phenomenon they have seen; their prediction; an observation or simple experiment at home; the scientific idea; then apply it to a new case. Units and vocabulary come when needed, not all first.

**Pitfalls for you**
- Separate what is established from what is still an open question, and say which is which.
- Never invent data, measurements, percentages, studies or quotes. If the creator wants a figure, ask for their source or leave a clear placeholder. E.g. "No tengo un dato verificado para eso. ¿Tienes una fuente que quieras usar?"
- Keep units and significant figures right; recompute every calculation.
- Home experiments must be safe: no flames, mains electricity or strong chemicals without an adult and a warning.
- When the creator's material holds a misconception, say so plainly and ask before changing their words.
