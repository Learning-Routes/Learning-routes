Use this to plan, write or review a business route.

**Where learners get stuck**
- Revenue taken for profit; costs that are not obvious (fees, taxes, their own time) left out.
- Building the product first and looking for customers later, without testing that anyone will pay.
- A long business plan taken as validation, instead of small experiments with real people.
- Price set by cost or by the competitor only, never by the value to the customer.
- Cash flow ignored: a profitable business can still run out of money.
- Success stories taken as the rule (survivorship bias).

**Exercises and blocks**
- scenario: a decision (raise the price, accept a client, hire) with the consequence of each option.
- simulation with a formula and sliders: profit = price × units − costs, break-even, margin.
- example: a fictional business worked through with numbers, labelled as fictional.
- drag_drop to order the steps of a process (validate, sell, deliver) or to sort costs into fixed and variable.
- fill_blank for key terms and simple calculations (match "exact" for numbers).
- check with options from the mix-ups above.

**Beginner sequence**: the customer and their problem; the offer and its value; a cheap test with real people; price and the basic numbers; selling; then measure and adjust. Each lesson ends with one small action in their own project, then a reflection on what happened.

**Pitfalls for you**
- Never promise or suggest income, results or timelines, not even as an example of what a student "will" earn. If the creator asks for that, explain kindly that the course can show the method and real examples, not results anyone can be sure of.
- No legal, tax or financial advice for a specific case. Taxes and laws change by country and over time: tell students to check with a professional, and ask the creator which country the course is for.
- Never invent market figures, success rates, companies' results or quotes. Fictional examples say they are fictional.
- Recompute every number in examples and exercises.
- Keep the creator's own cases and stories as they told them.
