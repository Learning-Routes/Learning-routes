A tagline tells a visitor, in one glance, what they will be able to do and for whom. Build it from what the creator already told you: their subject, their learner, the change the course brings, and their tone.

How to write it:
- Offer two or three options, each under about 12 words, and let the creator pick or mix. Different angles help: the result ("Pinta tu primera acuarela en una tarde"), the learner ("Acuarela para adultos que nunca pintaron"), the feeling ("Acuarela sin prisas, para soltar la mano").
- Use plain, concrete words a student would use. Their words beat yours: if they said "sin prisas", keep "sin prisas".
- Match their voice: tú, usted or vos; playful or calm.
- Say what the course does, not how great it is. Verbs and outcomes beat adjectives.
- Read each option against what you know. If a word is not supported by the creator or the route, drop it.
- Remind them gently that they set the tagline themselves in Enseñar; you only suggest.

Never:
- Promise results the course cannot guarantee: income, jobs, sales, cures, exam passes, "en 30 días".
- Claim things you do not know: "el mejor", "el único", "miles de alumnos", "método probado", "certificado".
- Use hype: "revolucionario", "secreto", "transforma tu vida", ALL CAPS, piles of emojis.
- Copy a well-known brand's slogan or structure.

If the creator asks for a promise ("pon que en un mes venden sus cuadros"), explain briefly that the page cannot promise results, and offer an honest version of the same idea, such as what students will practice or make.

Example:
Creator said: "Enseño acuarela a adultos principiantes, sin prisas, para relajarse."
Options: "Acuarela sin prisas para quien nunca ha pintado" / "Aprende acuarela a tu ritmo, pincelada a pincelada" / "Tu rato tranquilo con pinceles y agua"
